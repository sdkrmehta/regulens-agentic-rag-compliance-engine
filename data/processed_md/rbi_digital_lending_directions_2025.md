# Reserve Bank of India (Digital Lending) Directions, 2025

**Notification No.:** RBI/2025-26/36 (DOR.STR.REC.19/21.07.001/2025-26)
**Date of Issue:** May 08, 2025
**Regulator:** Reserve Bank of India
**Applicability:** All Commercial Banks, Primary (Urban) Co-operative Banks, State Co-operative Banks, District Central Co-operative Banks, Non-Banking Financial Companies (including Housing Finance Companies), All-India Financial Institutions (AIFIs), and their Lending Service Providers (LSPs) and Digital Lending Apps (DLAs).

---

## Chapter I: Preliminary & Applicability

### Clause 1. Short Title and Commencement
(a) These Directions shall be called the Reserve Bank of India (Digital Lending) Directions, 2025.
(b) These Directions consolidate, update, and supersede the Guidelines on Digital Lending dated September 02, 2022 (RBI/2022-23/111) and the Default Loss Guarantee (DLG) guidelines.
(c) These Directions shall come into force with immediate effect from May 08, 2025.

### Clause 2. Scope of Application
These Directions apply to digital lending extended by:
1. All Commercial Banks (including Small Finance Banks, Payments Banks, and Regional Rural Banks);
2. All Primary (Urban) Co-operative Banks, State Co-operative Banks, and District Central Co-operative Banks;
3. All Non-Banking Financial Companies (NBFCs), including Housing Finance Companies (HFCs);
4. All-India Financial Institutions (AIFIs);
5. Lending Service Providers (LSPs) and Digital Lending Apps/Platforms (DLAs) engaged by Regulated Entities (REs).

### Clause 3. Key Statutory Definitions
(a) **Annual Percentage Rate (APR):** The effective annualised rate charged to the borrower of a digital loan, computed based on an all-inclusive cost structure including cost of funds, credit cost, operating cost, processing fees, verification charges, and maintenance fees, excluding contingent charges like penal charges and late payment fees.
(b) **Cooling-off / Look-up Period:** The mandatory time window determined by the Board of the RE given to borrowers for exiting digital loans by paying the principal and proportionate APR without any penalty or foreclosure charge.
(c) **Digital Lending:** A remote and automated lending process, largely utilizing digital technologies for customer acquisition, credit assessment, loan approval, disbursement, recovery, and associated customer service.
(d) **Digital Lending Apps/Platforms (DLAs):** Mobile and web-based applications with user interfaces that facilitate digital lending services, including apps operated by REs as well as those operated by LSPs engaged by REs.
(e) **Lending Service Provider (LSP):** An agent of an RE who carries out one or more lender functions in customer acquisition, underwriting support, pricing support, servicing, monitoring, or recovery on behalf of the RE in conformity with extant outsourcing guidelines.
(f) **Default Loss Guarantee (DLG):** A contractual arrangement between an RE and an LSP or third party, whereby the guarantor compensates the RE for loss arising from loan defaults up to a pre-agreed percentage.

## Chapter II: Customer Protection and Conduct Requirements

### Clause 4. Loan Disbursal, Servicing and Repayment Mechanics
4.1 REs shall ensure that all loan disbursals and repayments are executed directly between the bank account of the borrower and the bank account of the RE without any pass-through account or pool account of any third party.
4.2 In no case shall loan disbursal or repayment be routed through a third-party pool account, including the collection accounts of LSPs or their DLAs.
4.3 Any exception is strictly limited to disbursals covered exclusively under statutory/regulatory mandate, flow of funds between REs for co-lending, and disbursals for verified end-use directly to the end-beneficiary.

### Clause 5. Prohibition on Direct Borrower Fee Deduction by LSPs
5.1 REs shall ensure that any fees, commissions, or charges payable to LSPs are paid directly by the RE and are NOT charged or deducted by the LSP from the borrower directly.
5.2 All penal charges levied for loan delay or default shall be based strictly on the outstanding amount and disclosed upfront on an annualized basis in the Key Fact Statement (KFS).
5.3 REs and their LSPs shall not charge penal interest capitalized into the loan principal.

### Clause 6. Disclosures and Standardized Key Fact Statement (KFS)
6.1 REs shall provide a standardized Key Fact Statement (KFS) to every digital lending borrower before the execution of the loan agreement.
6.2 The KFS shall prominently contain the Annual Percentage Rate (APR), recovery mechanism, details of the dedicated nodal grievance redressal officer, and the exact cooling-off / look-up period.
6.3 Any fee, charge, or expense not explicitly disclosed in the KFS cannot be charged by the RE to the borrower at any stage of the loan tenor.
6.4 Digitally signed copies of the KFS, sanction letter, terms and conditions, and privacy policies shall automatically flow to the borrower's registered email/SMS upon agreement execution.

### Clause 7. Multi-Lender Product Aggregation and Transparent Display
7.1 Where an LSP operates a platform aggregating loan offers from multiple REs, the LSP shall present an unbiased, uniform, and transparent digital view of all available loan offers to the borrower.
7.2 The DLA shall clearly display the names of all partner REs participating in the evaluation, clearly distinguishing between matched and unmatched lenders, ensuring that the borrower can make an informed choice without algorithmic steering.

### Clause 8. Cooling-Off and Look-Up Period
8.1 A borrower shall be given an explicit, unconditional option to exit a digital loan by paying the principal and the proportionate APR without any penalty, foreclosure fee, or exit charge during the cooling-off period.
8.2 The cooling-off period shall be determined by the Board of the RE, subject to the following statutory minimums:
- **Tenor of 7 days or more:** The cooling-off period shall NOT be less than three (3) calendar days.
- **Tenor of less than 7 days:** The cooling-off period shall NOT be less than one (1) calendar day.
8.3 Prepayment after the expiry of the look-up period shall continue to be allowed without penalty on floating-rate loans as per extant RBI regulations.

### Clause 9. Grievance Redressal and Nodal Officer Appointment
9.1 REs and their LSPs shall appoint a dedicated Nodal Grievance Redressal Officer to handle FinTech and digital lending complaints.
9.2 The contact details of the Nodal Officer shall be prominently published on the DLA, website, and within the KFS.
9.3 If a borrower complaint is not resolved within thirty (30) days, the borrower may escalate the complaint to the Reserve Bank-Integrated Ombudsman Scheme (RB-IOS).

## Chapter III: Technology and Data Requirements

### Clause 10. Collection, Usage and Sharing of Borrower Data
10.1 Data collection by DLAs must be need-based, minimal, and subject to prior, explicit, and audited consent from the borrower.
10.2 **Strict Mobile Resource Access Prohibitions:** DLAs are explicitly prohibited from accessing mobile phone resources, including file and media storage, contact lists, phonebooks, call logs, and telephony functions.
10.3 One-time access may be requested strictly for camera, microphone, or location, solely for customer onboarding and Video-based KYC (V-CIP) verification, with explicit informed consent.
10.4 Borrowers must be provided with an explicit option to grant or deny consent, restrict third-party disclosures, revoke previously granted consent, and request the app to delete/forget their personal data.

### Clause 11. Storage of Customer Data & Biometric Prohibition
11.1 LSPs and DLAs shall not store borrower personal information except basic operational data (name, address, contact coordinates) required for service delivery.
11.2 **Biometric Data Storage Prohibition:** REs shall ensure that no biometric data or raw fingerprint/facial templates are stored or collected on the servers or systems of DLAs or LSPs.
11.3 **Data Localization:** All personal, transactional, and operational digital lending data must be stored exclusively on servers physically located within India.

### Clause 12. Public Directory of Digital Lending Apps on CIMS
12.1 REs shall report all active DLAs operated by the RE or engaged via partner LSPs through the RBI Centralised Information Management System (CIMS) portal.
12.2 REs must ensure that every DLA offered to the public is validated against the RBI Public Directory of DLAs, preventing the circulation of unauthorized or predatory lending applications.

## Chapter IV: Default Loss Guarantee (DLG) Framework

### Clause 13. Default Loss Guarantee (DLG) Structure and 5% Portfolio Cap
13.1 Any Default Loss Guarantee (DLG) or First Loss Default Guarantee (FLDG) entered into between an RE and an LSP or third party shall adhere strictly to a maximum ceiling of five percent (5%) of the total outstanding loan portfolio.
13.2 DLG arrangements exceeding 5% of the portfolio are strictly prohibited and shall be treated as non-compliant synthetic securitization.
13.3 Every DLG arrangement must be backed by a legally enforceable contract and secured exclusively through cash deposits with the RE, fixed deposits maintained with scheduled commercial banks with lien marked to the RE, or irrevocable bank guarantees.
13.4 The eligibility and creditworthiness of the DLG provider must be audited by the RE at least once every financial year.
